A quick reference for the Spanish auction terms used across the dashboard.
          Spanish originals are shown in grey next to each term.


        

          
What you'd actually pay Art. 670 LEC

          
"Sin puja mínima" doesn't mean "any price wins". The court clerk (LAJ) only approves the award above legal thresholds, all measured against the Valor de subasta (the reference value):

          

            
          

          
Vivienda habitual (debtor's primary home) is stricter: nothing below 70% is approved, exceptionally 60%. The deposit is typically 5–10% of the auction value and counts toward your price if you win.


          
Where the money goes

          

            
You pay: your winning bidThe "remate". Your deposit counts toward it; the rest is transferred within ~20 days.

            
→ Creditor gets paidUp to the claimed debt (cantidad reclamada) plus interest & costs — out of your bid, not on top of it.

            
→ Surplus to the debtorAnything left after the creditor is satisfied (Art. 672 LEC).

            
+ You separately assumePrior charges (cargas anteriores) that survive, ITP transfer tax (~6–10%), notary/registry, and eviction costs if occupied.

          

          
The claimed amount is NOT your cost. It's the debtor's debt, paid out of your bid. Your real cost = winning bid + surviving prior charges + taxes + costs. Always read the nota simple for prior charges before bidding.

        


        

          
Auction type

          

            
Judicial Subasta judicial

              
Ordered by a court to enforce a debt — most often a mortgage foreclosure. The court (juzgado) runs it through the BOE portal. Auction IDs start with SUB-JA (forced, "en vía de apremio") or SUB-JV (voluntary).

              Court-run

            
Notarial Subasta notarial

              
Conducted by a notary, typically for out-of-court mortgage enforcement or certain voluntary and inheritance sales.

              Notary-run

            
Administrative Subasta administrativa

              
Run by a public body — usually the tax agency (AEAT) or Social Security — to collect public debts. Auction IDs start with SUB-AT.

              Agency-run

          

        


        

          
Reading BOE data

          

            
Auction ID SUB-XX-YYYY-NNN

              
The key that links a BOE notice to the auction portal. The two letters tell you the type: JA = judicial forced sale, JV = judicial voluntary, AT = tax-agency (administrative). Example: SUB-JA-2026-241891.

            
Forced vs. voluntary vía de apremio / voluntaria

              
"En vía de apremio" is a forced sale to settle a debt — the owner is being executed against, so occupancy and eviction risk matter more. "Voluntaria" is a court-supervised sale the owner asked for; usually cleaner.

              Apremio = higher risk

            
Where it's published Sección IV / V-B

              
Judicial auctions appear in the BOE under Section IV (Administración de Justicia); tax-agency auctions under Section V-B (Otros anuncios oficiales). The crawler reads both.

            
Auction ≠ tender subasta ≠ licitación

              
A "licitación" is a public procurement tender (awarding contracts), not a property auction. The BOE mixes both in its announcements; Auction Sonar keeps only true "subasta" notices and filters tenders out.

          

        


        

          
Occupancy Situación posesoria

          

            
Free of occupants Libre de ocupantes

              
The property is vacant, so taking possession after the award is straightforward.

              Best case

            
Occupied by the debtor Ocupado por el ejecutado

              
The former owner still lives there. You may have to go through an eviction to get possession.

              Eviction risk

            
Occupied by third parties Ocupado por terceros

              
Tenants or others occupy it. Their rights (e.g. a registered lease) may survive the sale.

              Check rights

            
Unknown No consta

              
Occupancy is not stated in the file. Verify on site before bidding.

              Verify

          

        


        

          
Status

          

            
Primary residence Vivienda habitual

              
The debtor's main home. It carries extra legal protection and changes the minimum award percentages under the LEC, so these deals are more sensitive.

              Sensitive

            
Viewable Visitable

              
Indicates whether the property can be visited before the auction. Often "no", which raises the unknowns.

          

        


        

          
Money & bidding

          

            
Auction value Valor de subasta

              
The reference/starting value the auction is based on.

            
Appraisal Tasación

              
The independent appraised market value used in the proceedings.

            
Claimed amount Cantidad reclamada

              
The outstanding debt being enforced. You do not pay this — it's settled out of your winning bid, with any surplus going to the debtor.

              Not a buyer cost

            
Deposit Importe del depósito

              
What you must pre-pay to be allowed to bid — typically 5–10% of the auction value. Refunded if you lose; counts toward the price if you win.

            
Minimum bid Puja mínima

              
The lowest accepted bid, when set. "Sin puja mínima" means no fixed floor — but the court still won't approve awards below the Art. 670 thresholds (≈50–70% of the auction value).

            
Entry 70/50% columna "Entry"

              
The table's realistic purchase price, computed from the auction value: top number = 70% (safe approval), below = 50% floor (60% for vivienda habitual). A real puja mínima, if set, raises the floor. The budget filter matches against the 70% figure.

              Use this for budgeting

            
Bid increment Tramos entre pujas

              
The step size bids must move in. A bid of, say, value + one tramo is the next valid offer above the current best.

            
Prior charges Cargas anteriores

              
Liens registered before the enforced debt survive the sale and transfer to you (Art. 670.5). Charges registered after are cancelled (Art. 674). Check the nota simple.

              You inherit these

            
Discount

              
How far the auction value sits below the appraisal: 1 − value / appraisal.

            
€/m²

              
Auction value divided by the surface — a quick price-per-square-metre.

            
Market estimate

              
A rough market value from comparable listings (median €/m² × area). Indicative only.

            
Upside

              
Estimated gap between the market value and the auction value, as a percentage.

          

        


        

          
Property & registry

          

            
Cadastral reference Referencia catastral

              
The 20-character ID of the property at the Catastro — the key to look up area, year and exact location.

            
IDUFIR / CRU

              
The unique ID of the registered property (finca) at the Land Registry.

            
Registry data Datos registrales

              
Land Registry coordinates of the property: finca number, tomo, libro, folio.

            
Charges Cargas

              
Liens and encumbrances on the property (mortgages, embargoes). Some may survive the sale — read the charges certificate carefully.

              Check carefully

          

        


        

          
Documents

          

            
Edict Edicto

              
The official auction notice with the legal terms and conditions.

            
Charges certificate Certificación de cargas

              
Land Registry certificate listing the property's liens and registered rights.

            
Appraisal report Valoración · Art. 666 LEC

              
The valuation report. The crawler also reads its stated surface to cross-check the cadastral area.
